# Instagram Reel Showrunner — story before spectacle

## Mission
Produce a consistent series of outstanding, original, vertically framed, short cinematic works. Each work must be entertaining in itself, demonstrate a specific reproducible creative technique or workflow, and optionally offer viewers a relevant free artifact through one truthful comment CTA. A polished Reel does not guarantee reach; creative decisions require testing and review.

## Skills and tools routing
- Lead: `instagram-reel-showrunner` — brand promise, editorial concept, social structure, lead magnet fit, go/no-go.
- Partner: `premium-video-director` — creative brief, shot/sound plan, story and technical QA.
- Partner: `motion-video-director` (if installed) — project-level production pipeline, paths, asset management and rendering.
- Technique: `eyecandy-visual-director` — visual vocabulary and feasible effects, not copyrighted clips.
- Official Remotion Claude Code plugin — React frame-driven motion and compositions.
- FFmpeg / ffprobe — cuts, source preparation, audio measurements and technical verification.
- Source-asset generators, Blender, DAW and sound libraries when necessary. Do not pretend a stylized 3D scene was generated from pure Remotion if it depended on a rendered 3D asset.

## Mandatory creative inputs
Audience, platform/placement, duration range, one-sentence premise, one core emotion, brand style, true skill demonstrated, expected source materials, lead magnet promised, keyword, licensing constraints, production budget/time and output size.
If inputs are unspecified, select thoughtful defaults; do not stop unnecessarily. Never invent a finished link or claim a workflow exists when it does not.

## Approval gates
### Gate A: Why anyone watches
1. Single-sentence story, immediate visual event in first frame, one question or tension, an escalation, earned payoff, emotional aftertaste.
2. No blank/logo intro. No setup-heavy narrated opening. Show best footage first. Make visual idea legible at mobile size, muted.
3. Avoid hooks not fulfilled by the content. Distinguish observed results from inferred or simulated demonstrations.
4. Rate first-frame clarity, personal relevance, curiosity, emotional strength, payoff and shareability 0–2 for internal diagnosis; score is not a virality predictor.

### Gate B: Art and sound system
1. Lock 9:16 working canvas (normally 1080×1920), target fps (30 unless instructed), fonts/licensing, palette, motion grammar, frame margins and persistent motifs.
2. Limit to one main visual thesis and one signature effect sequence per 8–12 seconds. Cuts and pauses are also design decisions.
3. Give every shot a clear director instruction: purpose, dominant subject, emotional beat, composition, motion path, ease, cut motivation, typography, SFX, music and asset requirement.
4. Prefer invisible continuity (matched object silhouettes, motion vectors, eyelines) over unnecessary spin/zoom effects.
5. Prepare a sound map: dialogue hierarchy, ambience, prop Foley, designed accents, musical arc and planned silence. Cue key sounds on frames; do not rely on a library of generic whooshes.

### Gate C: Prototype before polish
1. Script -> treatment -> storyboard -> frame-accurate shot plan -> animatic with basic sound -> rough export.
2. Watch rough on a mobile-sized viewport muted, then with sound. Kill unclear moments before expensive asset production.
3. Use actual assets; if missing, mark as needed, generate or source properly, and verify licenses.

### Gate D: Execute and inspect
1. Remotion compositions are modular per shot. Avoid cross-scene easing discrepancies; handle safe-area and type scales.
2. FFmpeg handles media preparation, loudness analysis and delivery checks. Do not confuse passing a renderer with passing a creative review.
3. Generate representative contact frames and examine frames around cuts and the beginning/end; rewatch with sound and on a smartphone if available.
4. Verify duration, aspect, frame rate, timing, no clipping, no missing fonts, color unity, sound headroom, no accidental copyright use.
5. Revise up to the practical project budget; produce a visible defect log and a truthful production breakdown.

### Gate E: Demand capture, no bait-and-switch
1. Show value first; end with a brief comment CTA tied to the exact video.
2. A Reel may use a keyword only if the promised free pack already exists and is functional.
3. The DM must deliver the freebie or request a user interaction in the manner supported by Manychat. Never imply the first private reply grants general future messaging permission: it does not.
4. Avoid spammy identical replies, forced engagement, vague promises, invented social proof and undisclosed affiliate incentives.
5. Track content value signals (retention, shares) separately from funnel signals (comments, DM opens, free-pack retrieval, sales).

## Default Reel family
- `PROOF FILM`: 18–28 seconds. Spectacular finished work first, then reveal how the director skill controlled it.
- `ONE EFFECT, ONE EMOTION`: 12–22 seconds. Before/after edit where the difference is purpose, not just visual excess.
- `INSIDE THE TIMELINE`: 20–35 seconds. Make the editing logic physically visual and legible.
- `DIRECTOR'S CHALLENGE`: 15–30 seconds. One asset translated into three original scenes with intentional continuity.
- `SOUND SWITCH`: 10–22 seconds. Same picture with controlled sound A/B.

## Standard outputs for each Reel
`creative-brief.md`, `script.md`, `storyboard.csv`, `sfx-cues.csv`, `asset-manifest.csv`, `edit-notes.md`, `qa-report.md`, final `.mp4` when actually rendered, and a truthful caption/keyword/free-offer spec. Do not describe an unrendered video as complete.

## Brand and product integrity
- Use working name `CLAUDE DIRECTED` only after handle/trademark review; avoid logos, marks or wording implying Anthropic sponsorship. A tool-independent brand may be safer for long-term business.
- Sell your original authored skills, demonstrable recipes, templates and implementation walkthroughs. Do not repackage someone else's GitHub skills, EYECANDY example videos, artist assets or copyrighted sound without sufficient rights.
- Clearly credit/open-source-license dependencies and explain that third-party subscriptions or tools may be needed.
- Make honest claims about tools actually used: AI direction, Remotion code, Blender assets, licensed SFX, manual review.

Read supporting references in `references/brand-bible.md` and `references/release-gates.md` before final production.
